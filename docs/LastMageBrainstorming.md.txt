## Last Mage — Brainstorming (Updated)

_A compact multiplayer party/hide-and-seek game set in a Roman-inspired fantasy world._

## Design Pillars
- **Chaotic:** Physics and player interactions create unpredictable moments.
- **Social:** Proximity chat and player interaction are central to the experience.
- **Risky:** Parkour rewards skill but creates opportunities for spectacular failure.
- **Escalating:** The mage grows stronger as the world becomes more dangerous.
- **Compact:** Small map, short rounds, high replayability.
- **Distinctive:** Rune-fire magic, a coastal fantasy setting, and dead-player interference create a unique identity.

## Core Concept
A fire mage hunts a group of small villagers through a dense seaside neighborhood. The mage grows stronger through sacrifice while a magical firestorm progressively consumes the map. Villagers must survive the timer or force the mage to fail before the firestorm overwhelms them.

## Player Count
- Target maximum: **6 players**.
- One player is the Mage.
- Remaining players are Villagers.
- Long-term mode: every player gets a turn playing the Mage, and total points determine the overall winner.

## Match Flow
1. Match begins.
2. Villagers receive a **30-second head start**.
3. The Mage does **not** know where Villagers spawned.
4. Villagers search for hiding spots, routes, chickens, and resources.
5. The Mage hunts chickens and villagers to gain power.
6. Firestorm zones begin advancing.
7. The required fire-control power increases as the match progresses.
8. The final stage becomes a chaotic last stand.

### Round Length
- Initial target: **8 minutes**.
- Subject to playtesting.

## Win Conditions
### Villagers Win
- Survive until the match timer expires.
- OR the Mage dies to the Firestorm.

### Mage Wins
- Kill every Villager before the timer expires.

## Character Scale
- Villagers: approximately 1 meter tall.
- Mage: approximately 1.4 meters tall.
- Sizes are intentionally small to create a funny visual style and make the map feel larger.

## The Mage

### Core Weapons and Abilities
- Small knife with low base damage.
- Short-cooldown ability that ignites the knife and increases damage.
- Longer-cooldown Fire Wave attack.

### Power System
Power only upgrades the Mage's fire magic.

Power increases:
- Fire damage.
- Fire range.
- Fire spread.

Power does **not** automatically improve all aspects of the character.

### Gaining Power
- Killing a chicken grants power.
- Killing a villager grants power.
- No ritual or sacrifice animation is required.
- A kill is considered the sacrifice.

### Power Decay
- Power effectiveness gradually decreases over time.
- The Mage does not lose unlocked power tiers.
- The Mage must continue hunting to maintain effectiveness.

## Chickens
- Chickens roam the map using basic pathfinding.
- Chickens create noise.
- Villagers can pick up and hide chickens.
- Hiding chickens denies the Mage an easy source of power.
- Chickens become an additional objective for both teams.

## Firestorm System
- The safe zone continuously shrinks.
- The Mage must maintain a minimum power threshold to control the magical firestorm.
- If the Mage falls below the required threshold, the firestorm overwhelms and kills them.
- Firestorm warnings should be clearly communicated.
- Firestorm progression should create decisions, not simply delete parts of the map.

## Movement and Parkour
### Design Goals
| Feature | Goal |
|----------|----------|
| Jumping | Responsive and simple |
| Climbing | Easy to understand but exposes players |
| Rooftops | Rewarding but risky |
| Falling | Funny and potentially costly |
| Collision | Creates slapstick moments |
| Mage Movement | Powerful but not automatically better |

### Parkour Philosophy
- Better hiding places are difficult to reach, not inherently safer.
- Skilled players gain access to stronger routes and hiding locations.
- Momentum should be a key part of movement.
- Physics should create funny accidents.

## Villager Counterplay
- Villagers cannot damage the Mage.
- Villagers can temporarily stun or disrupt the Mage.
- Movement, hiding, teamwork, and environmental interactions are their primary tools.

## Dead Player System
Dead players become agents of chaos rather than spectators.

### Camera
- Top-down view.
- Dead players cannot see highlighted players.
- They can identify players manually if they observe carefully.
- Limited vision radius rather than full-map visibility.
- Fog-of-war style information is preferred.

### Item Gameplay
- Dead players move around a separate overhead layer.
- Random items spawn.
- Dead players must collect items before throwing them.
- Thrown items that hit a player break.
- Missed items remain available to living villagers.

### Effects
- Primarily disruption and brief stuns.
- Dead players can help or sabotage either side.
- Multiple dead players can coordinate.
- Cooldowns are required to prevent excessive influence.

### Communication
- Dead players retain proximity voice chat.
- Possible future feature: megaphone communication system.

## Proximity Voice Chat
- Living players use proximity chat.
- Dead players also participate in voice chat.
- The Mage's voice is distorted.
- Distortion can intensify as power increases.

## Scoring System
### Mage Score
- Points for Villagers killed.
- Bonus points for a Mage victory.

### Villager Score
If Villagers lose:
- Points based on survival time.

If Villagers win:
- Win bonus.
- Additional survival-time score.

### Multi-Round Format
- Every player gets a turn as the Mage.
- Total points determine the overall winner.
- Point values are intentionally left open for balancing.

## Networking Goals
- Private online games with friends.
- Up to 6 players.
- No public matchmaking required initially.
- Networking implementation will be finalized later during Unreal development.

## Map Design Rules
- Every district must have multiple escape routes.
- Rooftop and street routes should connect.
- No hiding spot should remain safe permanently.
- The Mage should be able to reach most areas.
- Firestorm progression should create meaningful choices.
- The environment should encourage movement.

## Visual Identity
The game should balance charm and danger.

### Tone
- Small goofy characters.
- Dense colorful seaside architecture.
- Rune-emitting fire magic.
- Distorted supernatural Mage voice.
- Increasingly dangerous magical atmosphere.

The Mage should feel ominous without being physically gigantic.
